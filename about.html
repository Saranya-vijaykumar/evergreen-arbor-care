Our Heritage

Born in the Ancient Douglas Fir Forests

Founded in 2004 by ISA Board Certified Master Arborist Marcus Vance, ArborPro Operations originated as a specialized climbing unit tackling hazardous, storm-damaged trees that ordinary landscaping firms refused to touch.

Over two decades, we developed an industry-first hybrid approach: combining the rigorous academic diagnostics of botanical pathology with heavy-duty civil engineering rigging techniques. Today, we manage over 250,000 canopy acres for corporate campuses, municipalities, and private heritage estates throughout the Pacific Northwest.

Botanical Science in Practice

The CODIT Biological Defense Framework

Developed by Dr. Alex Shigo and formalized under ANSI A300 standards, the Compartmentalization of Decay in Trees (CODIT) model explains how living trees form four chemical and physical boundaries to isolate infections rather than heal damaged tissues like animals.

verified Our Pruning Standard
We strictly prune outside the branch bark ridge and collar. Never flush-cut, never top, preserving the natural barrier zone.
W1
Vertical Retardation

Wall 1: Upward / Downward

Resists the vertical spread of decay along conductive xylem vessels. The tree activates chemical plugs, tylose formations, and gum depositions to seal off longitudinal transport tubes above and below the wound.

arrow_upward_alt Weakest of the 4 Walls
W2
Centripetal Inward Defense

Wall 2: Inward to Pith

Resists inward spread towards the center heartwood. Composed of dense, thick-walled summerwood (latewood) growth rings and continuous tangential anatomical cells enriched with suberin.

compress Moderate Radial Resistance
W3
Circumferential Lateral Defense

Wall 3: Lateral Spread

Resists radial and circumferential spread around the trunk. Living ray parenchyma cells form chemical sheet barriers that prevent rot from encircling the tree's vital transport pathways.

sync_alt Strongest Pre-Injury Wall
W4
Barrier Zone (Post-Injury)

Wall 4: The Barrier Zone

The strongest barrier of all. Formed by the vascular cambium after injury, laying down a chemically impermeable layer of dense cells separating injured wood inside from brand-new, healthy wood grown outside.

verified Absolute Chemical Isolation
Regional Botanical Expertise

Pacific Northwest Native Species Field Guide

Every Pacific Northwest canopy species has unique soil microbiology relationships, storm resistance dynamics, and physiological disease vulnerabilities. Here is how our certified arborists formulate species-specific care protocols.

PNW Keystone Conifer

Western Red Cedar

Thuja plicata
Up to 200 ft
Primary Vulnerability: Summer drought stress, top dieback, shallow root windthrow in saturated soils.
ArborPro Care Protocol: Sub-surface pneumatic irrigation, mycorrhizal inoculation, crown sail-area reduction.

Western Red Cedars lack taproots and depend heavily on shallow, moisture-retentive duff layers. We apply specialized arborist wood chips and slow-release organic humates to simulate old-growth forest floor ecosystems.

Canopy Giant

Coastal Douglas Fir

Pseudotsuga menziesii
Up to 250 ft
Primary Vulnerability: Laminated root rot (Phellinus sulphurascens), red ring rot, severe winter gale force gusts.
ArborPro Care Protocol: Sonic tomograph root flare inspection, aerodynamic wind-firming, deadwood removal.

Our region's dominant structural timber. We engineer precise end-weight reduction on long lever-arm lateral branches to prevent structural failure during Pacific Northwest bomb cyclones.

Pacific Hardwood

Bigleaf Maple

Acer macrophyllum
Up to 100 ft
Primary Vulnerability: Extreme epiphytic moss wet-weight loads, narrow codominant crotches with included bark.
ArborPro Care Protocol: Dynamic Cobra synthetic cabling, branch union stress relief, selective moss load reduction.

Bigleaf Maples carry up to 1,500 lbs of water-saturated moss during PNW rainy seasons. We install non-invasive shock-absorbing flexible cables that prevent massive leader splits without drilling metal bolts into living wood.

Sensitive Heritage Broadleaf

Pacific Madrone

Arbutus menziesii
Up to 80 ft
Primary Vulnerability: Fungal cankers (Neofusicoccum), Phytophthora root disease, summer lawn irrigation root suffocation.
ArborPro Care Protocol: Strict dry-root rhizosphere isolation, phosphite micro-infusion, sterile tool sanitization.

One of the most fragile and protected species in the Puget Sound ecosystem. We establish strict no-trenching buffer zones and treat branch cankers with systemic organic fungicides before infections breach the trunk.
